# VECTRA-X Project Competitiveness Audit

## Executive Assessment

VECTRA-X is technically stronger than a conventional competition submission because
it does not stop at disease classification. It demonstrates multi-label reasoning,
explicit leakage control, uncertainty, co-infection detection, fairness stress
testing, patient triage, and resource planning. Its strongest defensible competition
claim is not that it has the highest possible score; it is that the reported pre-lab
score is clinically honest and embedded in a usable decision-support workflow.

Verified headline results:

- Pre-lab Extra Trees: macro F1 0.647, micro F1 0.840, weighted F1 0.848.
- Pre-lab macro recall 0.725 and macro PR-AUC 0.608.
- Full-feature research track: macro F1 0.705, inflated partly by target-restatement leakage.
- Co-infection detector: ROC-AUC 0.863, PR-AUC 0.871, recall 0.835.
- Conformal prediction: 0.948 empirical coverage, average set size 2.90.
- Leave-one-center-out macro F1: approximately 0.38.

## 1. What Is Currently Strong

### Exploratory data analysis

- Correctly identifies the task as multi-label: 158 of 300 patients have more than
  one active diagnosis.
- Quantifies severe label imbalance and explains why accuracy is inappropriate.
- Includes label cardinality, co-occurrence, co-infection profiles, missingness,
  demographics, associations, and PCA.
- Connects EDA findings to modeling choices rather than presenting disconnected charts.

### Modeling

- Uses multi-label stratified splitting and out-of-fold model selection.
- Benchmarks linear, bagging, boosting, and classifier-chain approaches.
- Separates pre-lab, lab-aware, and full-feature research tracks.
- Reports macro, micro, weighted, and samples F1; Hamming loss; subset accuracy;
  Jaccard; per-label precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC.
- Uses per-label threshold policies instead of assuming 0.5 is universally valid.

### Interpretability and responsible AI

- Provides global and per-label permutation importance, local explanation examples,
  and optional SHAP.
- Adds calibration, uncertainty, conformal prediction, subgroup auditing, and
  leave-one-center-out robustness.
- Explicitly warns that anonymized features are statistical signals, not medical causes.

### Dashboard and prototype

- Provides working patient selection and patient reports.
- Runs saved-model batch inference on original-schema CSV files without retraining.
- Supports model-mode and threshold-policy selection.
- Includes population filtering, resource capacity simulation, downloads, and
  artifact fallbacks.
- Makes limitations and non-diagnostic status visible.

### Competition scoring perspective

The project has a coherent problem-solution-contribution chain. The leakage audit is
especially valuable because it turns a likely jury attack into evidence of mature
methodology.

## 2. What Is Weak or Risky

### Data limitations

- Only 300 patients and two health centers.
- Yellow fever has 12 positives and only three positives in the held-out test.
- Chikungunya, Zika, and option 8 have zero positives and cannot be modeled.
- No timestamp or usable patient geolocation for outbreak forecasting.
- Several clinical and laboratory variables have heavy missingness.

### Model limitations

- Pre-lab typhoid F1 is 0.308 with false-negative rate 0.714.
- Yellow-fever metrics are unstable because support is extremely small.
- Malaria prevalence is 90%, limiting the model's ability to rule it out.
- Lab-aware performance does not uniformly improve over pre-lab performance.
- Conformal prediction sets are broad, with about 95% containing multiple labels.

### Leakage concerns

- `Dengue (Dengua)` nearly restates the dengue target.
- Laboratory fields are valid only after tests are available.
- A judge may challenge why the full model is shown. The answer must be that it is a
  controlled leakage demonstration, never the proposed triage model.

### Metric concerns

- The test set contains only 77 patients.
- Rare-label point estimates have wide uncertainty.
- Out-of-fold and held-out results must be labeled consistently during presentation.
- Accuracy should not be used as the main success claim.

### Dashboard limitations

- Cohort patient triage artifacts are generated by the pre-lab model; switching mode
  changes verified track metrics and upload inference, not the historic cohort table.
- Uploaded inference does not reproduce cohort conformal sets or the separate
  co-infection model because those fitted artifacts were not persisted as deployable bundles.
- Local patient explanation text is not available for every patient.

### Presentation and jury attack points

1. "Is this a diagnostic tool?" No. It is triage decision support.
2. "Why are prediction sets so large?" The dataset is small, symptoms overlap, and
   malaria is highly prevalent; the system communicates this ambiguity rather than hiding it.
3. "Why is lab-aware not always better?" The available tests mainly confirm already
   common malaria and do not guarantee improved rare-label discrimination.
4. "Can it generalize?" Not yet. Center-transfer performance shows prospective
   validation and recalibration are required.
5. "Are explanations medically causal?" No. They explain model behavior only.

## 3. Improvements to Maximize Winning Chance

### A. Quick wins

- Use the operational threshold policy in the main demonstration and show safety as a scenario.
- Demonstrate one patient each for confident, co-infection, uncertain, urgent, and
  false-negative-risk cases.
- Lead with the pre-lab model and show the leakage demonstration only after establishing trust.
- Add confidence intervals through bootstrap resampling for key held-out metrics.
- Persist calibrated upload inference and co-infection model bundles in the next pipeline revision.

### B. Medium improvements

- Add nested or repeated multi-label cross-validation to quantify score variance.
- Add decision-curve or net-benefit analysis for confirmatory-test prioritization.
- Calibrate per facility and compare center-specific thresholds.
- Add explicit data-drift checks for uploaded batches.
- Conduct a clinician or public-health-worker usability review of triage actions.

### C. Advanced, high-risk/high-reward improvements

- Collect additional rare-label and multi-center data.
- Develop hierarchical or label-dependency models with uncertainty-aware ensembling.
- Add prospective temporal validation.
- Add geospatial and climate enrichment only after reliable timestamps and locations exist.
- Validate conformal methods designed specifically for multi-label and imbalanced settings.

### D. Presentation and storytelling improvements

- Open with the problem: overlapping symptoms, delayed tests, and co-infection.
- Reveal the leakage trap visually and explain why the honest model matters.
- Demonstrate a patient moving from probability to uncertainty to triage action.
- Finish with the resource simulator to show humanitarian operational value.
- Avoid claiming deployment readiness; claim a validated competition prototype and a
  clear prospective-validation pathway.

### E. Technical report improvements

- Replace reference placeholders with a consistent citation style.
- Add bootstrap confidence intervals and sample-support annotations to rare-label tables.
- Include an explicit model card and data statement.
- Add a short ablation table for calibration, conformal, and triage layers.
- Document uploaded-inference scope separately from cohort research outputs.

## 4. Comparison With Likely Competitors

| Likely competitor approach | VECTRA-X advantage |
|---|---|
| Generic XGBoost classifier | Multi-label benchmark with stage-specific model tracks |
| Accuracy as headline metric | Macro F1, PR-AUC, recall, calibration, and per-label analysis |
| All columns used together | Explicit leakage and feature-availability audit |
| Single forced prediction | Conformal prediction sets and uncertainty-aware review |
| No co-infection handling | Separate co-infection detector and multi-label output |
| Static charts or report | Saved-model upload inference and interactive resource simulation |
| No fairness analysis | Subgroup recall gaps and center-transfer stress test |
| No operational layer | Four-tier triage and capacity-gap calculation |

## 5. Recommended Final Competition Framing

### Main model

Present the **pre-lab Extra Trees model**. It is the most credible answer to the
humanitarian early-triage problem.

### Secondary model

Present the **lab-aware XGBoost model** as confirmation support after tests are ordered.
Do not imply that it is necessarily superior across every label.

### Full-feature model

Present it only as a controlled leakage experiment. Explain that the team intentionally
included it to demonstrate how easily medical AI performance can be overstated.

### Defending the leakage audit

The audit shows understanding of prediction timing and clinical workflow. A model is
not deployable merely because a column exists in the dataset. Feature availability at
decision time is part of the model specification.

### Positioning the dashboard

The dashboard is a humanitarian response prototype that combines patient triage,
manual-review deferral, batch intake, population burden, and capacity planning. It is
not a consumer diagnosis interface.

## Final Recommendation

Do not chase a slightly higher score by weakening the leakage boundary. The winning
framing is: **VECTRA-X reports the model that could actually be used at triage time,
knows when it is uncertain, and converts predictions into transparent humanitarian
response priorities.**
